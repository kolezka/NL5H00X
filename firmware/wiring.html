NL5H00X UART bridge wiring

Four direct wires from the projector board to the ESP32-C6 SuperMini. No resistors or capacitors needed. Flash the ESP32 over USB first, then solder.

Wires

ProjectorESP32-C6Note
TX pad20Projector output into the ESP32.
RX pad19The firmware releases this pin while the projector sleeps, so it never drives an unpowered SoC.
IR VCC3V33.3 V. Never on the 5V pin, never while USB is plugged in.
IR GNDGNDSupply ground and UART ground in one wire.

Order of work

  1. Fill firmware/include/secrets.h, including OTA_PASSWORD. Flash the loose ESP32 over USB.
  2. stage 1Solder projector TX to 20 and IR GND to GND. Keep the ESP32 on USB from the Mac.
  3. Connect with nc projector-uart.local 2323, log in, turn the projector on. You should see readable boot text or console:/ $. No text at all? Move the wire to the other pad. Pin 20 is an input, so that is safe.
  4. stage 2Unplug USB. Solder 19 to projector RX, and IR VCC to 3V3.
  5. Turn the projector on. Connect, check that the banner shows no BROWNOUT, press Enter and type id.
  6. Put the projector in standby and wake it with the remote. This proves the ESP32 does not starve the IR supply.

Later updates

No cable needed. In the browser, open http://projector-uart.local/, pick firmware/.pio/build/esp32-c6-supermini/firmware.bin under Firmware update, enter the OTA password and upload. Or from the terminal:

cd firmware
PROJECTOR_OTA_PASSWORD='…' pio run -e ota -t upload

The value must match OTA_PASSWORD in secrets.h. If a new build breaks Wi-Fi, disconnect the 3V3 wire and flash over USB. GND, TX and RX can stay soldered.

Status page without login: http://projector-uart.local/

Do not
  • Plug in USB while IR VCC is on 3V3. The ESP32 regulator would push 3.3 V into the projector's standby rail. Pulling the projector's mains plug does not help.
  • Use the 5V pin. With 3.3 V in, the regulator leaves about 3.0 V and the ESP32 resets under Wi-Fi load.
  • Use ESP32 pin 16 (marked TX on some SuperMinis). The chip prints its own boot log there.
